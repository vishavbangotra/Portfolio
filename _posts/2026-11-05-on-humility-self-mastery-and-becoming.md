---
title: On Humility, Self-Mastery, and Becoming
topic: Philosophy
description: Nine notes on the ego, the poison of praise, and the self you once thought impossible.
---

1\.
{: .numeral}

Leave the ego behind. Leave the excitement behind, and the sweet habit of admiring yourself. These are the ornaments of a man who has stopped climbing. Become capable. Become formidable. Become exceptional. But the moment you believe this places you above another, you have already begun to fall.

2\.
{: .numeral}

Be great in your actions. Remain humble in your perception of yourself. The deed is for the world. The image is for you alone, and it is the easier of the two to inflate.

3\.
{: .numeral}

Admiration is a drug, and so is criticism. One lifts you and one lays you flat, and both leave you dependent on the hand that gave the dose. Neither is a measure of who you are. They measure only the one who speaks. Be intoxicated by neither.

4\.
{: .numeral}

Beware even your own thoughts of greatness. When you are low, the mind is cunning: it manufactures a superiority to shield you from the feeling of not being enough. This is not insight. It is self-preservation dressed as judgment. Do not take shelter in that inflated image. A man who hides in his own flattery has found a comfortable prison.

5\.
{: .numeral}

Whoever labours to be respected has made the crowd his master. He has given others the keys to his inner life, and now they decide when he may feel accomplished. Do not hand that power to anyone. Your effort, and the satisfaction it leaves behind, must not wait on how others see you.

6\.
{: .numeral}

Measure your work by your own honest reflection. Ask: did I push myself toward the difficult thing? Did I confront what I was avoiding? Did I give my best to what needed doing, even when it was uncomfortable, even when it brought no reward? Here, and nowhere else, is the satisfaction that does not rot.

7\.
{: .numeral}

To be true to your work is to be true to yourself. Do what must be done according to your own values and your own understanding of what is right, not what is popular, not what is convenient, not what is most likely to be applauded. Follow your instinct. Act with integrity. And do not let any of this harden into contempt. Retain your love for the world.

8\.
{: .numeral}

Do not live to be admired. Do not work to be validated. Do not let praise, criticism, or the expectations of others take hold of the reins. The direction is yours, or it is no direction at all.

9\.
{: .numeral}

Become the version of yourself you once thought impossible. Not to prove your superiority. Not to earn recognition. Not to stand above anyone. Only to discover how far you can go, because no one has ever told a man the full extent of what he is capable of becoming. He must find it for himself.
