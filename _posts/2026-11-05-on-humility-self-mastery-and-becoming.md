---
title: On Humility, Self-Mastery, and Becoming
topic: Philosophy
description: Seventeen notes on the forged coin of self-admiration, the tavern of praise, the only honest scale, and the distance still to travel.
---

## Of the Mirror

1\.
{: .numeral}

Excitement, self-admiration, the warm glow of one's own name: these are the first things the climber leaves at the foot of the mountain. Not because they are sins. Because they are heavy.

2\.
{: .numeral}

“I am great,” says the man who has just been made small. Watch when this thought visits you. Not in the hour of victory, when you are too busy to notice yourself. It comes in the hour of inadequacy, at night, after the failure, when the floor gives way. The mind is a merciful forger. It mints a counterfeit superiority so that you need not feel the poverty underneath. Do not spend that coin. It buys nothing but sleep.

3\.
{: .numeral}

A man's thoughts of his own greatness are his most loyal courtiers, and therefore his most dangerous.

4\.
{: .numeral}

Be great in what you do and humble in what you take yourself to be. The order matters. Whoever reverses it, vast in self-regard and meagre in deed, has built a palace on a painting of a foundation.

## Of the Tavern

5\.
{: .numeral}

Praise and blame are two wines from the same cellar. One makes you dance, the other makes you weep, and in both cases you are no longer standing upright. Who would take his measure from the noise of a tavern?

6\.
{: .numeral}

When you are praised, ask who is speaking. Another point in the vastness, looking from another angle, wanting something you cannot see. His admiration tells you about his hunger. His contempt tells you about his fear. Neither tells you anything about you.

7\.
{: .numeral}

He who works to be respected has sold his evenings in advance. Others will now decide whether he may rest content, whether the day was good, whether he is enough. A strange bargain: to labour a whole life, and then hand the wages to the crowd to distribute as it pleases.

8\.
{: .numeral}

Not to live to be admired. Not to work to be validated. Not to be steered by anyone's expectations. This is not arrogance. Arrogance still has its eyes on the audience. This is the turning away of the eyes.

## Of the Only Scale

9\.
{: .numeral}

What remains, once praise is silenced and blame ignored? The oldest court there is: your own honest reflection at the end of the day, with the lamp out and no one listening.

10\.
{: .numeral}

It asks three questions, and none of them is “did it go well?”

> Did I go toward the difficult thing?
>
> Did I confront what I was avoiding?
>
> Did I give my best to what had to be done, even when it was uncomfortable, even when it paid nothing?

11\.
{: .numeral}

The satisfaction those answers leave behind never needs to be shown to anyone. That is how you know it is genuine. Every other kind requires a witness.

12\.
{: .numeral}

Whoever is true to his work is true to himself. There is no second loyalty. Do what must be done by your own values and your own sense of what is right. Not by the popular, which is the opinion of many who have not thought. Not by the convenient, which is the road of those who wish to stay as they are. Not by the applauded, which is the herd congratulating itself.

## Of Height Without Rank

13\.
{: .numeral}

But beware the solitary who turns sour! He who frees himself from the herd and then despises it has only traded one dependence for another. He still needs the herd, now as the thing beneath him. Keep your instinct. Keep your integrity. And keep, which is the hardest of the three, your love for the world.

14\.
{: .numeral}

There is height, and there is rank. Height is how far you have climbed from where you began. Rank is where you stand next to another. Seek the first without limit. Refuse the second entirely. The cedar does not think itself above the grass. It only grows.

## Of Becoming

15\.
{: .numeral}

Somewhere behind you stands a younger self who once said, “That, I could never be.” Go back and see what he was pointing at.

16\.
{: .numeral}

Become the one you once thought impossible. Not to prove your superiority, for whoever needs proof has already doubted. Not to be recognised, for recognition comes late and understands little. Not to stand above anyone, for there is no above. Only this: to find out how far a human being can go once he stops asking for permission and stops asking for applause.

17\.
{: .numeral}

What you are is not a fact to be defended. It is a distance to be travelled.
