---
title: On Trusting Oneself
topic: Philosophy
description: Eight notes on instinct, the herd, and walking toward the thing you avoid.
---

1\.
{: .numeral}

Nobody stands above you. Nobody stands below you. Every man is a point in a space too vast for any one eye to measure, and no point is the summit. Whoever looks up in reverence has forgotten his own height. Whoever looks down in contempt has forgotten his own fall.

2\.
{: .numeral}

Seek what is singular in you. It is not a virtue you must earn. It was given when you were made, like a face, and only the cowardly spend their lives in disguise.

3\.
{: .numeral}

The herd is warm, and that is its whole argument. It offers company in exchange for your name. Whoever asks the crowd “what am I?” will receive the answer the crowd gives itself, and he will call this wisdom because it was said by many mouths.

4\.
{: .numeral}

Praise is a leash with a pleasant sound. Blame is a leash with an unpleasant one. Both are held by hands that stand at another point in the space, looking at another horizon, and an equal who hands an equal a verdict on how to live does not give light. He gives distortion. Take counsel as you take weather, noting it without obeying it. Let the decision remain yours alone.

5\.
{: .numeral}

What does the instinct say, before the chorus begins? Listen to that. It is old, older than your doubts, and it has never once asked permission.

6\.
{: .numeral}

Trust is not a feeling that arrives before the deed. It is what the deed leaves behind. He who waits to believe in himself before he acts will wait until the grave.

7\.
{: .numeral}

Become the wolf. Not for cruelty, but because the wolf does not ask the flock whether it may hunt. Become a man with a mission, and let the mission be the hardest one: to find yourself, to build yourself, to master yourself.

8\.
{: .numeral}

Where does mastery begin? Ask what you fear. Ask what you have been walking around for years, and what you pretend not to see. That is not an obstacle on your road. That is your road. Go toward it. Self-confidence is not a gift of the gentle. It is the scar of the man who stopped avoiding.
