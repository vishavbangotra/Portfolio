---
title: Effort is the only thing I keep
topic: Philosophy
description: On a nightly journal borrowed from Seneca, and why it never asks how the day went.
---

Every night I answer three questions before I sleep. What did I put effort into today? Where did I hand my attention to things I can't control? What will I do tomorrow that is mine to do?

None of them ask whether the day went well. That is deliberate. Outcomes belong to too many people — a reviewer, a release train, an interviewer having a bad morning. Effort belongs to me.

Seneca reviewed each day back to himself every evening, once the lamp was out. I borrowed the habit during a stretch when work felt like pushing the same deploy uphill. <mark>It did not make the hill smaller. It made the pushing legible.</mark>

> The hill does not get smaller. You get stronger, and the climb becomes yours.

Some nights the honest answer is: not much. That's allowed. The page doesn't cheer and it doesn't scold. It keeps the record, and the record is enough to start again.

What I've noticed, a few months in, is that the third question does most of the work. Naming tomorrow's one thing — small, specific, mine — turns the morning from a negotiation into a decision.
