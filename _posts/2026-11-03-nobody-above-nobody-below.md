---
title: Nobody above, nobody below
topic: Philosophy
description: On trusting your instincts, why other people's opinions turn into noise, and walking straight at the thing you're avoiding.
---

For a long time I treated my instincts as a first draft. Something to check with someone else before I acted on it. A friend, a senior, a forum thread. I asked for opinions the way some people ask for permission.

Then I started to picture people differently. Not on a ladder, where someone is always a rung above you and someone a rung below, but as points in a three-dimensional space. Everyone is somewhere. Nobody is higher. Nobody is lower. Each person is at their own point, facing their own direction, carrying their own mix of what they're good at and what they're still running from.

<mark>Once you see it that way, the ladder disappears, and so does the reason to keep looking up it for approval.</mark>

## Opinions become noise

Here is where I've landed. If everyone is an equal, then an opinion from another person is just one equal handing a verdict to another. They are standing at a different point. They see from a different angle. Their opinion tells you about where *they* are, not about where you should go.

So I've stopped treating opinions as instructions. Taken in too often, they don't sharpen your view. They blur it. They pull your attention towards someone else's values and away from your own. That is what I mean by noise: not that people are wrong, but that their verdict on your life was never theirs to give.

> Nobody is above you. Nobody is below you. That is where trusting yourself begins.

## Trust, then act

Trusting your instincts starts with knowing what is yours. What makes you different, the way you think, what you notice that others walk past. Once you can see that clearly, your instinct stops feeling like a guess and starts feeling like information.

And trust changes behaviour. When you rely on your own judgement, you stop waiting. You act. You stop running every decision past a committee. The herd keeps moving in whatever direction the herd moves; you get to choose a path of your own.

I've started thinking of it as becoming the wolf rather than one more sheep. A man on a mission. And the mission isn't a job title or a number. It is to identify yourself, build yourself, and master yourself.

## What mastery means

Mastery, for me, isn't polish. It is honesty about your edges. Where are your limits? What scares you? What have you been quietly stepping around for months?

That last question is the useful one. The thing you are avoiding is almost always the thing you most need to do. So go towards it — deliberately, and on purpose. Not because someone told you to, and not to prove anything to anyone, but because it's the only direction that actually moves you.

That is how self-confidence gets built. Not from being told you're good enough. From walking straight at what you were avoiding, and finding out you are still standing on the other side.

Learn to trust yourself. Make your own path. Then go for the exact thing you've been avoiding.
