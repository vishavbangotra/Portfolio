---
title: On Becoming
topic: Philosophy
description: Eight notes on humility, the drunkenness of praise, and the self you once thought impossible.
---

1\.
{: .numeral}

Leave the ego at the gate. Leave the excitement too, and the quiet pleasure of admiring yourself in your own thoughts. They are heavy things to carry on a road that climbs. Strive to become capable, formidable, exceptional, and never once believe this lifts you above another man. The mountain does not look down on the valley. It is only higher, and it is still made of earth.

2\.
{: .numeral}

Be great in your deeds and humble in your image of yourself. The first is a matter of work. The second is a matter of honesty. Most men have it backwards: great in the mirror, small in the deed.

3\.
{: .numeral}

Praise and criticism are both wine. One is sweet and one is bitter, and a man can get drunk on either. Neither one measures what you are. Each measures only where the speaker stands and what he hoped to see. Drink neither. Keep your head clear for the work.

4\.
{: .numeral}

Distrust even your own voice when it speaks of your greatness. When you are low, the mind builds a throne out of nothing and seats you on it so that you need not feel the floor. That is not judgment. It is a bandage. A man who lives inside his bandage never learns whether the wound has healed.

5\.
{: .numeral}

Whoever works for admiration has handed over the keys to his house. Now others decide when he may feel proud, when he may rest, when he has done enough. Do not give strangers that power over your inner life. Do not live to be admired. Do not work to be validated. Your effort, and the satisfaction it leaves behind, must stand on their own feet.

6\.
{: .numeral}

Then by what measure? Only your own honest reckoning when the day is done. Did I push toward the difficult thing? Did I face what I was avoiding? Did I give my best to what needed doing, even when it was uncomfortable, even when it earned nothing? These questions do not flatter, and that is why they can be trusted.

7\.
{: .numeral}

To be true to your work is to be true to yourself. Do what must be done by your own understanding of what is right, not by what is popular, not by what is convenient, not by what will be applauded. Follow the instinct. Keep your integrity. And keep your love for the world. The man who has learned to despise everyone has not escaped the herd. He has only found a lonelier one.

8\.
{: .numeral}

Become the self you once thought impossible. Not to prove that you are superior. Not to be seen. Not to stand on anyone's shoulders. Only to learn how far a man can go. The distance between who you were and who you could become is the only height worth measuring.
